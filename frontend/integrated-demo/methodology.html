How climate-model changes are applied
Tmean_future = Tmean_historical + (ΔTmax + ΔTmin) / 2
DTR_future = DTR_historical × monthly model DTR ratio
Tmax_future = Tmean_future + DTR_future / 2
Tmin_future = Tmean_future − DTR_future / 2
Rain_future = Rain_historical × monthly future/reference rainfall ratio

DTR is the daily temperature range. Monthly factors compare each model’s future period with that same model’s historical reference. Missing factors stop the simulation rather than becoming zero or “no change.” Weather is adjusted for each model independently before simulating rotations.

Future scenarios keep the historical order of wet, dry and hot days. They do not predict new storm sequences, exact future heatwave dates or changed rainfall occurrence. GPM, SMAP and MODIS are not used in this runtime.
02 / THE DAILY MODEL

Rain adds water. Crops draw it down.

A single soil-water store continues through the entire rotation, without resetting at each crop.

Crop water demandETc = Kc × ET0

Weather determines reference evapotranspiration (ET0); the crop coefficient (Kc) changes through the modeled crop calendar.

Water available todayA = yesterday’s store + rain

Actual modeled ET cannot exceed either crop demand or available water.

Water shortfallUnmet demand = ETc − actual ET

This is a model output, not measured irrigation need or crop damage.

Exact equations and units

Reference ET: FAO-56 Hargreaves

ET0 = max(0, 0.0023 × (Tmean + 17.8) × √(Tmax − Tmin) × 0.408 × Ra)

Temperatures in °C; Ra in MJ/m²/day, converted to equivalent mm/day using 0.408. ET0 and ETc are in mm/day. Historical and future runs use the same method.

Daily water balance

A = S + P
ETa = min(ETc, A)
U = ETc − ETa
D = max(0, A − ETa − C)
S_next = A − ETa − D

S is stored available water; P is precipitation; C is available-water capacity; ETa is actual modeled ET; U is unmet demand; D is excess drainage. All quantities are in mm. Storage stays within 0–C and daily mass is conserved.

Stress and intensity

depletion = C − min(S + P, C)
water stress: depletion > p × C (while a crop is active)
intensity = max(depletion − p × C, 0) / ((1 − p) × C)
heat exceedance = max(Tmax − crop heat threshold, 0)

The existing crop depletion fraction p is used after rainfall but before daily ET. Intensity ranges from 0 to 1 and is zero in the degenerate p=1 case. It does not reduce ET in this simple bucket. Heat exposure uses Tmax ≥ the existing screening threshold, so a threshold-equality day has exposure but zero exceedance.

03 / WHY THAT ROTATION?

Your constraints first. Your priorities next.

We use the existing backend comparison — no new frontend resilience score.

Keep feasible options

Must-grow crops, avoided crops, available equipment, stored rotation rules and calendar compatibility filter the alternatives. Constraints are not relaxed to produce more options.

Your original sequence is retained as the reference, even when it conflicts with new constraints.

Compare the outcomes

Potential demand, unmet water demand and sensitive-window water/heat indicators are assessed alongside qualitative nitrogen demand and calendar-derived time with a living crop.

A lower actual ET caused by stress is not a water-saving benefit.

Use your priorities

The backend normalizes comparison metrics within the candidate set, then applies the supplied priority weights. Future scoring uses independently simulated model-median outcomes.

The highlighted pick uses the backend’s balanced label or highest returned score. It is not a universal best rotation or a probability of success.

What nitrogen means here

Low, medium and high are assumed ordinal demand categories. The model averages crop categories and applies its documented label cutoffs. It does not calculate kg N/ha, fertilizer savings, nitrogen losses or emissions.

No predecessor nitrogen credit is applied: supporting citations are incomplete.

How to read the rotation timeline

Year 1 → Year 2 → Year 3 follows the crop order. The blue line is soil water remaining at the end of each day; brown bars are crop water demand the model could not meet. Water carries continuously across crops and years.

The bottom lanes mark sensitive-period water stress, heat exposure and their overlap. This is one labeled model and reference-weather cycle adjusted to future conditions, not predicted future dates. Days/year totals summarize the full record and all starting positions; they are not crop damage.

Why a sequence can be unavailable

The fixed corn harvest and winter-wheat planting dates can overlap. We keep the entered sequence and report the calendar limitation rather than inventing a comparison.

Cover-crop water demand is not parameterized, so rye and clover cannot be included in scored simulations yet.

04 / SCOPE AND LIMITS

A screening tool, not a field prediction

Modeled

Regional future climate sensitivity, continuous soil water, crop demand, water shortfall, recurring stress windows and feasible rotation comparisons.

Approximate assumptions

Fixed crop calendars, sensitive periods, heat thresholds, representative soils, 1 m depth, 50% initial storage and a one-year warm-up. All starting rotation phases use the same initialization policy.

Not modeled

Irrigation, tile drainage, snow accumulation/melt, frozen-soil infiltration, runoff, off-crop evaporation, cover-crop water demand, yield, profit, measured fertilizer savings or erosion.

Decision support, not agronomic advice. Model agreement is not probability; results are not farm-level validation.
